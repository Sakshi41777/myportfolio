How I Manage College, Projects, and Learning New Tools Together

By Sakshi Mishra | 4th Sem B.Tech AI & DS

As a 4th-semester B.Tech student specializing in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, managing college academics while building real-world projects and exploring new tools can often feel like a juggling act. However, with the right structure and mindset, it becomes manageable—and even enjoyable.

In this blog, I want to share how I’ve been balancing coursework, personal development, and project-building, all while staying consistent with my learning goals.

🎓 1. Setting Academic Priorities First

Being consistent with classroom learning has helped me build a strong foundation in subjects like:

  • Operating Systems
  • Database Management Systems
  • Software Development with Agile Methodology
  • Mathematics
  • Data Science (RGPV curriculum)

I align my project work with these subjects wherever possible. For instance, my Inventory Management System directly connects with my DBMS concepts.

🛠 2. Breaking Projects into Weekly Goals

Instead of viewing a project as one huge task, I break it down into smaller weekly targets. My current method includes:

  • Listing features/modules in Notion
  • Assigning 3–4 small tasks per week
  • Tracking time spent using simple logs

This helps me maintain steady momentum without being overwhelmed.

💡 3. Leveraging AI Tools the Smart Way

I use tools like ChatGPT, Power BI, Google Colab, and Kaggle not to skip the learning, but to accelerate it.

  • For code debugging: ChatGPT helps me understand errors faster.
  • For dashboards: Power BI helps me visualize data and make my work presentable.
  • For datasets: Kaggle is my go-to resource.

Using AI tools wisely helps me learn by doing instead of just consuming content.

🗓 4. Creating a Weekly Routine

I maintain a flexible schedule that includes:

  • 🧠 2 hours for learning new concepts (e.g., Pandas, SQL joins)
  • 🔧 2–3 hours for applying them to a project
  • 📚 Study blocks for core subjects
  • ✍️ A weekend slot to reflect or write (like this blog!)

This balance keeps me moving forward across all areas.

💬 5. Asking for Feedback Regularly

Sharing my work early with peers, mentors, or even LinkedIn connections helps me get valuable insights. I learn faster when someone reviews my work, asks questions, or suggests alternatives.

Feedback is not a correction—it’s a shortcut to growth.

🚀 Final Thoughts

The tech world moves fast, but learning doesn’t have to be rushed. I’ve realized that consistent small actions are more powerful than occasional big efforts.

As I continue to grow in this field, I aim to:

  • Explore more real datasets
  • Improve my understanding of ML algorithms
  • Build tools that solve actual problems

Thanks for reading! Connect with me on LinkedIn.
